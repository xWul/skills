# Copy review & experiment protocol

## Editorial scorecard (not a performance prediction)

Assign 0–5 to each dimension, justify briefly, and total out of 40:

1. Audience relevance
2. Immediate clarity
3. Benefit specificity
4. Credibility / proof
5. Differentiation
6. Emotional fit / respectful tone
7. Offer transparency
8. CTA and next-step clarity

Explain that subjective scores are useful for prioritizing revisions, **not evidence** of conversion lift or the likelihood of sales. Do not label any score “guaranteed to work.”

## Audit output

- **What works:** 1-3 specific strengths, with evidence from actual material.
- **What breaks persuasion:** prioritize no more than 3 issues.
- **Rewrites:** show ready-to-use improvement for the most serious issue.
- **Hypotheses:** state why each revision could work, and what would disprove it.
- **Test plan:** event to optimize for, one meaningful variable per comparison when feasible, reasonable observation period and sufficient data; do not invent statistical confidence.

## Interpreting ad funnel metrics

Impressions/reach → link click → landing page view → initiated checkout → purchase.

- High CTR, low landing page views: investigate load speed, destination mismatch, accidental clicks, tracking.
- Healthy page views, few checkout starts: investigate offer, clarity, trust, price, UX.
- Checkout starts without purchases: investigate payment friction, shipping/taxes (if physical), trust, abandoned flow; don't claim precise cause without evidence.
- Poor CTR: test clearer hooks, audience-message fit, creative variation.

Check measurement windows, sample sizes, attribution and whether data are comparable before stating that metrics improved or degraded.
